ATS-Friendly Resume: How to Make a Resume That Gets Read

Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) help hiring teams process large volumes of applications. Here is what ATS software actually does, how parsers read your text, and how to format a resume that stays clear for both machines and human reviewers.
By Deskwork • September 28, 2026 • 8 min read

If you have applied for jobs online recently, you have almost certainly encountered an Applicant Tracking System (ATS). From corporate career portals to third-party recruitment platforms, ATS platforms are widely used across industries to organize incoming applications.

A lot of myth and anxiety surrounds ATS software. Some online claims suggest automated robots make all hiring decisions in secret or that you can "trick" algorithms with invisible text. The reality is much more mundane: an ATS is primarily a database and search tool designed to extract text, organize applicant profiles, and help human hiring managers review candidates efficiently.

No tool or template can guarantee that your resume will be accepted or shortlisted by an ATS, because hiring decisions depend on your real qualifications and the company's specific selection criteria. However, following sensible formatting and text clarity practices ensures that your document can be parsed smoothly without garbled text or lost sections.

1. What ATS Software Does

When you submit a resume through a job portal, the ATS performs two main functions:

  • Document Parsing: The software attempts to strip away visual styling and extract raw textual data into structured profile fields, such as candidate name, phone number, email address, job titles, employers, employment dates, degrees, and specific skill terms.
  • Search & Filtering: Recruiters and talent acquisition specialists query this internal database using keywords, job titles, location, and years of experience to identify relevant profiles for upcoming openings.

If a resume's structure is too convoluted or relies on unreadable decorative elements, the parser might misidentify dates, scramble job titles, or overlook valuable skills entirely.

2. Why Readable Formatting Matters

The primary rule of ATS-friendly resume writing is simple: what is easy for a human recruiter to scan quickly is almost always what is easiest for automated parsers to digest.

A readable resume uses standard hierarchical structure: contact information at the top, a concise summary, followed by clearly separated sections for experience, education, and technical capabilities. When sections follow an expected sequence, software parsers can accurately assign each block of text to its appropriate database field.

Furthermore, once an application makes it onto a recruiter's shortlist, an actual person will read it. A layout that looks clean, uncluttered, and well-proportioned on a monitor creates a positive impression immediately.

3. Choosing Clear Resume Headings

One of the easiest ways a resume gets scrambled in an ATS is when candidates invent creative or unconventional section headers. Parsers rely on predictable heading patterns to identify where one category of information ends and another begins.

Stick with conventional headings such as:

  • Professional Summary (or Summary, Career Objective)
  • Work Experience (or Professional Experience, Employment History)
  • Education (or Academic Background)
  • Skills (or Technical Skills, Core Competencies)
  • Certifications & Licenses

Headings like "What I Bring to the Table," "My Journey So Far," or "Milestones" may sound creative, but software parsers frequently fail to recognize them, leading to omitted experience or unindexed skills.

4. Using Job-Description Keywords Naturally

Keyword matching is a standard function in applicant tracking tools. Recruiters frequently search their applicant pools using terms directly mentioned in their job advertisements.

However, "keyword stuffing"—repeating a phrase ten times or pasting the entire job listing into tiny font at the bottom of the page—is not only counterproductive, it is easily flagged by modern systems and immediately rejected by human reviewers.

Instead, integrate keywords naturally:

  • Analyze the job posting: Note recurring tools, methodologies, and technical requirements (e.g., financial modeling, Python, client onboarding, Scrum, Google Analytics).
  • Reflect your genuine experience: If you have used those specific tools or processes, incorporate the exact terms directly into your bullet points and skills list.
  • Include both acronyms and full terms: Write out both forms where appropriate, such as Search Engine Optimization (SEO) or Certified Public Accountant (CPA), ensuring whichever term the recruiter searches for matches your profile.

5. Fonts and Formatting

Exotic, custom, or heavily decorative fonts often lack embedded vector outlines or can cause encoding glitches when converted into plain text. Stick to standard, universally available system fonts or clean web-safe typefaces.

  • Recommended fonts: Arial, Calibri, Helvetica, Georgia, Times New Roman, Trebuchet MS, or IBM Plex Sans.
  • Font sizes: 10 to 12 pt for body copy; 14 to 16 pt for section headings; 18 to 22 pt for your name.
  • Margins: Keep standard 0.5 to 1.0 inch margins around the document to ensure balanced spacing when printed or viewed on small screens.

6. Tables, Columns, Graphics and Unusual Layouts

Many visually elaborate graphic templates utilize complex multi-column grids, text frames, floating shapes, and image-based skill bars. While visually striking in a design portfolio, these layouts can present significant challenges for automated resume parsers:

  • Text boxes and floating frames: Some text extraction engines process floating text boxes out of order or ignore them completely, which may cause your contact details or sidebars to disappear.
  • Multiple nested tables: Older parsing scripts read multi-column tables horizontally left-to-right across the whole page, rather than down each column individually, interleaving unrelated words.
  • Graphic skill meters: Graphic bars displaying "80% proficiency" or 4 out of 5 stars cannot be converted into meaningful textual data by a parser.

If you want a safe, widely compatible format, a well-structured single-column or clean dual-section layout with native text delivers the highest parsing accuracy.

7. Writing Strong Experience Bullets

Once a recruiter views your parsed or original resume, the quality of your bullet points determines whether you receive an interview invitation. Strong experience bullets follow an active, result-oriented formula:

Action Verb + Context/Task + Measurable Result

Weak: "Responsible for managing social media accounts and creating posts."
Strong: "Managed three corporate social media channels, publishing 12 monthly content campaigns that increased community engagement by 28% over two quarters."

Action-oriented bullets demonstrate both the scope of your responsibilities and your practical impact in the organization.

8. Common ATS-Related Mistakes

Avoid these frequent pitfalls when submitting resumes to automated application portals:

  • Submitting image-only files: Saving your resume as a PNG, JPG, or rasterized PDF prevents parsers from reading any selectable text. Always ensure text can be highlighted with your cursor.
  • Hiding contact info in header/footer zones: Some word processors store headers and footers in separate XML layers that certain parsers skip. Place your name and contact details at the very top of the main body text.
  • Using non-standard bullet characters: Fancy arrows, diamonds, or custom icons can render as strange symbols or question marks. Use standard round solid bullets or simple dashes.
  • Applying with one generic resume: Sending identical resumes to dozens of completely different job postings means you rarely align with the specific vocabulary of any single position. Lightly customize your summary and highlighted skills for each role.

9. Final ATS-Friendly Resume Checklist

Before submitting your application, verify these key items:

  • ✓ Your name, email, phone number, and location are in the main body, not locked inside isolated page headers.
  • ✓ Headings use standard, easily recognizable terms (Work Experience, Education, Skills).
  • ✓ Important industry keywords and tools from the job listing are included naturally where applicable.
  • ✓ Text is fully selectable and copy-pasteable.
  • ✓ No critical information is stored solely inside images, icons, or graphic rating bars.
  • ✓ The document is exported as a clean, standardized PDF or DOCX file as requested by the employer.
