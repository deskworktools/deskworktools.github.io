How to Write a Rirekisho (履歴書) for a Japan Visa Application

If you're applying for a job or a visa in Japan - especially under programs like Specified Skilled Worker (SSW) or Technical Intern Training - you'll almost certainly be asked for a rirekisho (履歴書), Japan's standardized resume format. It looks nothing like a Western CV, and getting the structure wrong can genuinely hurt an application, even when the person's actual qualifications are strong.

Why the Format Matters So Much

Employers and immigration reviewers in Japan expect the rirekisho in one consistent structure. It's less about creativity and more about completeness and correctness - every section has a reason for being there, and reviewers notice when one is missing or filled in incorrectly. Treat it less like "designing a resume" and more like "correctly completing a form."

What a Rirekisho Actually Needs

A complete rirekisho covers far more ground than a typical resume:

  • Basic Information - your name in furigana, date of birth, permanent address, marital status, and a recent passport-style photo
  • Physical & Health Information - height, weight, dominant hand, blood type, eyesight, tattoo status, smoking/drinking habits, and general medical history
  • Academic Background - listed by start and end year (not just graduation year), including school name, specialty, and any license or qualification earned
  • Language Proficiency - JLPT level, JFT-Basic, NAT Test, or similar, with your actual score if you have one
  • Work History - same start/end year format, company name, and a short description of your role
  • Other Information - hobbies, personality traits, and religious or dietary restrictions if relevant to the role (common for caregiving and hospitality positions)
  • Family Structure - required for most visa categories (not typical for a standard job application) - names, relationships, ages, occupations, and whether they live with you

Common Mistakes That Get Applications Rejected

  • Skipping the physical and health section - it's not optional for most sponsored visa categories
  • Writing dates in month-day-year order - Japanese forms consistently expect year-month-day
  • Leaving the family structure section blank when applying for a residency or training visa
  • Submitting a generic Western-style resume instead of the expected rirekisho format entirely
